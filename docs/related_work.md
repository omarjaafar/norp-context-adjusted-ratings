# Related Work Notes

## McConville & Cordery (2021): critique of financial ratios
- Argues: Standard financial-ratio measures can be misleading for nonprofits because they often treat organizational capacity and mission context as if they were comparable across settings. Ratios can penalize organizations serving high-need populations or operating under structurally constrained conditions.
- Relation to our pipeline: This is the clearest motivating theory for the project. Our pipeline tests whether naive ratio-based ratings unfairly disadvantage nonprofits in high-need counties, and then adjusts for county context and organizational differences before deciding whether a result is robust.

## Carnochan et al. (2013): performance measurement challenges
- Argues: Nonprofit performance is difficult to measure because organizations have multiple missions, heterogeneous outputs, and weak comparability across settings. A single metric can easily misrepresent performance when it ignores mission, operating environment, and stakeholder goals.
- Relation to our pipeline: This supports the project’s emphasis on a structured theory-testing workflow rather than a single score. The pipeline is designed to test hypotheses across multiple dimensions, use controls, and apply a validation gate so that weak or misleading indicators do not pass as evidence.

## Treinta et al. (2020) and Miller-Stevens et al. (2021): multidimensional frameworks
- Argues: Nonprofit performance should be evaluated using multidimensional frameworks rather than simple financial ratios alone. Financial, operational, mission, and contextual performance are interrelated and should be assessed together.
- Relation to our pipeline: This aligns directly with the pipeline’s design: the planner identifies a theory, the data scout gathers relevant fields, the executor tests multiple statistical models, and the validator checks whether effects survive confounder controls and replication criteria. The project is explicitly building a framework for evaluating complex nonprofit outcomes rather than relying on a one-dimensional rating.

## Nordin et al. (2022): capacity vs. social performance
- Argues: Organizational capacity and social performance are distinct constructs. An organization can look financially weak or less efficient while still delivering meaningful social value in a difficult context, especially when demand is high and resources are constrained.
- Relation to our pipeline: This motivates the project’s county-level and subgroup analyses. The pipeline is designed to distinguish between apparent financial weakness and true social or operational performance by comparing organizations across county need, sector, and size and by checking whether a result is context-dependent.

## Tuckman & Chang (1991): financial vulnerability
- Argues: Nonprofit financial vulnerability is linked to liquidity, operating strain, and dependence on external resources, not simply to whether an organization appears “efficient” by standard accounting measures. Vulnerability can arise from structural conditions, not just managerial failure.
- Relation to our pipeline: This offers a substantive benchmark theory that can be translated into a hypothesis in the system. It fits the project’s broader goal of distinguishing between conventional financial ratings and deeper measures of organizational risk or stress, especially in places with high service need.
